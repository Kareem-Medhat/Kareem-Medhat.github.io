What it tests and when to use it

3DMark runs game-like 3D scenes to measure your graphics card (GPU). This guide covers two of its tests: Time Spy and Steel Nomad. Each test has its own score, so only compare a Time Spy score with other Time Spy scores.

Use it after installing a new graphics card or driver, or when games run slower than expected. Scores are in points (pts). Higher is better.

Before you start

  1. Save your work. Close other apps, including browsers, game launchers and chat apps. They use processing power and lower your score.
  2. On a laptop, plug in the charger. On battery, most laptops slow down to save power.
  3. Install a temperature monitor. We use HWiNFO (free). It shows how hot your CPU and GPU get during the test.
  4. On Windows 11, set the fastest power mode: Settings → System → Power & battery → Power mode → Best performance.
  5. Restart the PC, then leave it idle for 5 minutes. Windows often runs updates and background tasks right after startup.

Download

3DMark is made by UL Solutions. Get it from UL's official 3DMark download center or the official 3DMark page on Steam. Don't use other download sites.

Install and set up

  1. If you use Steam, open the 3DMark store page and install it like a game. Otherwise, download the installer from UL's 3DMark download center and run it.
  2. Start 3DMark.
  3. If 3DMark offers to download extra test files, allow it for Time Spy and Steel Nomad.

Run the test

Run each test with its default settings, so your result matches the reference scores.

Time Spy

  1. Open the list of benchmarks and choose Time Spy (not Time Spy Extreme).
  2. Select Run.
  3. Don't use the PC while the test runs. It takes a few minutes.

Steel Nomad

  1. Open the list of benchmarks and choose Steel Nomad (not Steel Nomad Light).
  2. Select Run.
  3. Don't use the PC while the test runs.

Read your result

Time Spy shows an overall score plus a Graphics score and a CPU score. Use the Graphics score: it measures your graphics card on its own.

Steel Nomad shows one score. Use that number.

3DMark may offer to show your result online on UL's website. That's UL's service; nothing is sent to us.

Troubleshooting

My score is low

  1. Go through Before you start again. Background apps and a battery-saving power mode are the most common causes.
  2. Run the test again with HWiNFO open. If the temperature climbs to its limit and the clock speed drops, that's thermal throttling (see below).
  3. Make sure your monitor cable is plugged into the graphics card, not into the motherboard ports.
  4. Update your graphics driver from your GPU maker's website (NVIDIA, AMD or Intel).
  5. Turn off overlays and screen recorders (for example game-launcher overlays) before you run the test.
  6. Run the test two or three times. Small differences between runs (a few percent) are normal.

It crashes, freezes or the PC restarts

  1. Undo any overclock or undervolt. The simplest way is to load the default settings in your BIOS. (The BIOS is the setup screen your PC shows before Windows starts.)
  2. Update your graphics driver from your GPU maker's website (NVIDIA, AMD or Intel).
  3. If the PC shuts off completely under load, your power supply may not be strong enough for your graphics card.
  4. Check temperatures. Overheating can cause sudden shutdowns.

Thermal throttling

Thermal throttling means your processor or graphics card slows itself down because it's too hot. It protects the hardware, but it lowers your score.

  1. Clean out dust from fans, filters and heatsinks. (A heatsink is the metal block with fins that pulls heat away from the chip.)
  2. Check that every fan spins while the test runs.
  3. Give the case room to breathe: don't push it against a wall or put it in a closed cabinet.
  4. On a laptop, use it on a hard, flat surface, not a bed or a couch.
  5. If temperatures are still high, the cooler may not be mounted properly. Ask someone experienced or a repair shop to check it.
